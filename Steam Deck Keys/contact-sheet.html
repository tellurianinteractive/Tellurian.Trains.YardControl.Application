Command Pad Keyfaces

Twenty icons drawn from the yard diagram's own EBICOS geometry, so a key is a picture of the thing on the screen rather than a pictogram to learn separately. Set the language, turn the words off, shrink to real size — that is the test.

Every icon

Files are in Steam Deck Keys/svg/, each a 96-unit viewBox on a transparent ground, with no text anywhere except the sample train number.

Why the word underneath is the verb

A 96 px key on a ~19 mm face gives about 5 px/mm. Comfortable reading across a layout room wants a cap height of at least 3 mm, which is roughly a 20 px font — and at that size a condensed face fits about ten characters, a normal-width one about eight. Every object noun blows that budget in at least one language. Every action verb fits, which is why the icon carries the noun and the word carries the verb.

Danish, Norwegian and German drafts are mine and want your eye before they go near a resource file. Swedish follows Messages.sv.resx, except that the point positions use Rakt / Sido from your own key sketch rather than the resx's Avvikande. That difference is itself the argument for a separate ButtonLabels.resx: button vocabulary wants to be shorter and more idiomatic than message vocabulary.

Palette

Every colour is EbicosColours, unchanged, so a key and the diagram can never show two different greens for the same route. Two values sit outside the palette, on the same grounds Platform and GridLine already do.

Notes for the composer

One signal size, everywhere

A small pierced triangle is not a small signal — EBICOS gives it its own meaning, the stored route start point (SignalView.StartPointFlag). So every main signal and dwarf chevron on every key is drawn at 34 units, whatever else shares the key. The route icons set the ceiling: two symbols and the link between them have to fit across 96. This is why the icons are generated rather than hand-drawn, and why this page reads its geometry from the SVG files instead of repeating it.

Icon zone and label zone

Each icon is centred and sized for the full 96 box. When a label is composed under it, scale the icon to 0.63 and align it to the top, leaving the bottom 37% for text. One Skia transform, and the SVGs stay usable on their own.

Use a condensed face

Labels here are Barlow Semi Condensed. Condensed buys roughly 20% more characters at the same cap height, which is the difference between Ablenkung fitting and not. Whatever face you register with SkiaSharp, make it a condensed one.

No domain tint baked in

The icons carry no background. If the pad turns out to want your orange row tint, that is a compose-time fill under the icon — one line, changeable in an afternoon, rather than twenty files to redraw.

Pre-render every variant

Fill-key-with-colour replaces the image rather than tinting it, so each state and the alternate hairline needs its own encoded JPEG. Encode them all at start-up and hold the bytes; repaint is then transfer only.

Watch the green

#008000 is dark. It is right on a screen and it is what the diagram uses, but on an emissive key in a dim room it may want lifting. Judge it on the hardware — and if it changes, change it for the diagram too.

The train number is a sample

The four lifecycle icons draw 2145 as a picture of “a train number”, not a real one, and use four digits rather than TrainNumberDisplay's padded five so it stays legible at 19 mm.
